Why This Matters

AI is actively reshaping nuclear medicine education for technologists. Understanding where we are on the curve — and where the hype ends — is the foundation of effective integration into your curriculum.

Context
The Gartner Hype Cycle
▼
Applications
Four Domains of AI Use in Nuclear Medicine Technologist Training
▼
  • Clinical — Patient communication simulations, imaging artifact analysis, positioning guides, and competency checklists
  • Educational — Drafting syllabi, board prep (NMTCB/ARRT) modules, quiz generation, and explainer animations
  • Research — Synthesizing literature for student projects, designing data collection workflows, and abstract drafting
  • Administrative — Streamlining JRCNMT accreditation documents, creating grading rubrics, and drafting student recommendations
Ethics
The Challenge of Accuracy & Safeguards
▼

AI models hallucinate — they generate confident, plausible, but factually incorrect content. In nuclear medicine education, this has real stakes for student competency.

  • Always verify AI-generated study materials against primary textbooks
  • Teach students critical evaluation over passive reliance
  • Establish clear academic integrity policies for student AI usage
  • Student and patient data must never be entered into public LLMs
Note
Important Caution — All AI Tools
▼
No student data, clinical identifiers, or protected health information (PHI) should be uploaded to or entered into any AI tool without explicit permission and in accordance with your institutional policies, FERPA, and applicable HIPAA regulations.
02

Anatomy of a Prompt

Prompt engineering is the art and science of crafting precise instructions for AI models to generate desired responses. The better your prompt, the better your educational output.

Definition
Everyday Analogies
▼

As an educator, you already know how to prompt — you just don't call it that:

  • A well-structured grading rubric
  • A precisely worded lesson plan objective
  • A clinical competency evaluation form
  • Clear instructions for a student lab assignment

Prompt engineering applies the same precision to generative AI.

Framework
The 6 Elements of an Effective Prompt
▼
Role
Who should the AI act as? (e.g., "You are an expert NMT program director...")
Task
What specific action should it perform? Clear verbs matter. (create, summarize, draft, formulate)
Context
What background does the AI need? (student year level, certification exam focus)
Example
Optional but powerful — provide a sample of what you want (e.g., a past exam question).
Format
How should the output be structured? (bulleted study guide, multiple-choice, table)
Tone
What register? (academic/technical for syllabi, plain language for patient communication scenarios)
Worked Example — Educator Context
▼
Role
You are an experienced nuclear medicine technologist educator.
Task
Create a concise study module about gamma camera quality control (QC).
Context
The students are first-year technologists preparing for their initial clinical rotation. They struggle with the difference between uniformity and resolution.
Example
For the quiz section, format questions like this: "Q: Which daily test ensures the camera sees a uniform field of radiation? A) Center of Rotation B) Uniformity Flood"
Format
Use a structured bulleted list. Include definitions, common artifacts, daily vs. weekly QC tests, and a short 3-question quiz at the end.
Tone
Encouraging, clear, and highly focused on practical clinical application.
03

Prompt Techniques

Five techniques that dramatically improve AI output quality — specifically tailored for drafting exams, writing syllabi, or building training materials.

Technique 1
Free Text Prompting
▼

The foundation — available in every AI platform. Apply the 6-element anatomy and iterate.

  • Refine your prompt based on each output to narrow the focus
  • Start a new chat if the context becomes confused
  • Critique outputs and explicitly ask the AI to "try again and fix X"
  • Use "forget history" where supported
Technique 2
Role-Playing Prompts
▼

Assign the AI a specific expert persona to improve accuracy and calibrate tone for your student audience.

"You are an NMT program director reviewing an accreditation self-study document. Identify gaps relative to current JRCNMT standards..."
Technique 3
Chain-of-Thought Prompting
▼

Force the AI to reason step-by-step before reaching a conclusion. Critical for generating complex physics explanations or grading rubrics.

  • Ask AI to "explain your reasoning step-by-step"
  • Define each reasoning step explicitly for student feedback
  • Example: walking through complex radiopharmacy calculations
Technique 4
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
▼

Ground AI output in your documents — not just its training data. Upload a syllabus or textbook chapter and prompt against it.

  • Upload your specific program guidelines or lecture notes
  • Generate study guides, summaries, or NMTCB-style Q&A grounded in the source
  • Every claim is traceable back to your uploaded document
Technique 5
Custom AI Tools (GPTs & Gems)
▼

Build or use pre-configured AI assistants trained on specific instructions — no prompting from scratch each time.

  • Custom GPTs (ChatGPT) and Gems (Gemini) save reusable system prompts
  • Build a "Student Recommendation Letter Writer" or "Exam Question Generator"
  • Share within your program faculty for consistent outputs
  • No coding required — configure via natural language
